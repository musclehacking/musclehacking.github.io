<p>If you really wanted to change, you’d do it now.</p>
<p>You wouldn’t put it off until tomorrow.</p>
<p>You wouldn’t secretly hope that you forget about it until you’re already halfway into your dessert.</p>
<p>You wouldn’t set vague goals like “<em>I want to lose weight</em>” or “<em>I want a six-pack</em>”.</p>
<p>If you were serious about wanting to change your life, you’d do it <em>now</em>.</p>
<p>Not tomorrow.</p>
<p>Not after <em>&lt;insert holiday&gt;</em>.</p>
<p>Not once you’re done with <em>&lt;insert pathetic excuse&gt;</em>.</p>
<p> Now.</p>
<p>Handballing all of the work involved in reaching your goals to your future self is knowingly indulging
    in
    self-delusion.</p>
<p>You’re banking on a future version of yourself that’s somehow more capable, more motivated, and more
    disciplined than
    you are today.</p>
<p>How exactly does this future version of yourself become more capable and disciplined than you are right
    now? Through
    eating cake and watching Netflix? That’s your plan?</p>
<p>Might as well make them a couple feet taller while you’re at it.</p>
<p>This is a fundamental misunderstanding of how change works. It’s a convenient fiction, allowing you to
    absolve
    yourself of responsibility while maintaining the illusion of commitment.</p>
<p>The truth is, your future self is only going to find it <em>harder</em> to make the change.</p>
<p>Because you’re—</p>
<ol>
    <li>Forming a habit of opting for instant gratification and;</li>
    <li>Setting the precedent that this is your <em>future self’s</em> problem.</li>
</ol>
<p>Real change requires immediate action.</p>
<p>A commitment to do the hard things <em>today</em>. Not tomorrow.</p>
<p>Waiting for a magical shift in circumstances or motivation is a recipe for stagnation, not
    transformation.</p>
<p>The path to genuine transformation is paved with the small, consistent choices you make every day, not
    the grand
    declarations of change for some indefinite future.</p>
<p>It’s about confronting the discomfort of growth head-on, rather than seeking shelter in the comfort of
    old habits.
</p>
<p><mark>Each day you choose delay, you not only lose a day’s progress but also reinforce the belief that
        change
        is something
        beyond your current grasp.</mark></p>
<p>By convincing yourself that your future self will somehow possess the willpower and discipline you lack
    today, you’re
    not only engaging in a fantasy but also actively disempowering your present self.</p>
<p>The power to change is most potent in the present moment. It’s not about a complete
    overhaul in a
    single day but rather the incremental progress that accumulates over time.</p>
<p>Imagine if, instead of postponing, you took one small step towards your goal today. Then another
    tomorrow.</p>
<p>Before long, you’d look back to see a series of steps leading up to a significant distance covered. This
    is how
    lasting change is forged—not in the leaps and bounds of our fantasies but in the steady, persistent
    march of
    reality.</p>
<p>Your future self is not a superhero, endowed with magical powers of will and discipline.</p>
<p>It is merely you, plus the effects of the choices you make today.</p>
<p>If you want to make life easier for that future self, the best thing you can do is start making those
    hard choices
    now.</p>
<p>Begin with one small, manageable change. Make it again tomorrow. And the day after.</p>
<p>The path towards the change you desire
    begins <em>now</em>,
    with you, in this moment. This. Moment.</p>
<p>Not with grandiose plans for tomorrow, but with the concrete actions you take today.</p>
<p>Let today be the day you stop deferring your dreams to an idealised future self.</p>
<p>Embrace the discomfort and <em>just do it</em>.</p>
<p>After all—</p>
<blockquote>
    <p>The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago. The next best time is today.</p>
    <p>- Chinese
        proverb</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Why are you still sitting here reading Chinese proverbs?</p>
<p>Get to work.</p>
